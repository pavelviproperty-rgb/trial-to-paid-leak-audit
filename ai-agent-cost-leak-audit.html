The ugly number

Most teams know monthly AI spend. Fewer know cost per successful workflow, retry-heavy workflow, or workflow that never should have kept running. That is where flat pricing breaks: one loop, one power user, or one revoke bug can erase the margin of many normal users.

For AI SaaS

Fixed or hybrid pricing, usage-based LLM costs, and unclear margin by user, workflow, or plan.

For agent teams

Multi-step chains with retries, tool calls, memory, long context, file parsing, browser actions, or fragile stop conditions.

For automation operators

Client or internal workflows where one bad prompt, retry loop, or stale entitlement state can turn a profitable contract into a loss.

What we check

  • Cost per completed workflow, not only cost per token.
  • Top expensive prompts, traces, users, workflows, and task types.
  • Retries, tool-call loops, oversized context, and memory injection.
  • Model routing mismatch: expensive models on cheap tasks.
  • Hard-stop vs soft-warning guardrails.
  • Billing, entitlement, and revoke-state drift where relevant.

What you get

  • Executive verdict: healthy, underwater, or unknown.
  • Top 3 workflow leaks and the fastest fixes.
  • Budget-guardrail decision table: hard stop, soft warn, reconcile later.
  • Pricing, cap, and margin warnings.
  • Instrumentation checklist for the next 48 hours.

What this catches fast

  • A workflow that completes rarely but spends heavily on retries.
  • Cheap-tier users routed through premium models by default.
  • Tasks that look profitable in revenue but fail on gross margin.
  • Soft budgets that warn but do not actually stop overnight burn.
  • Usage or access state that drifts after budget limits are hit.

Why this is not another AI cost dashboard

  • This starts from outcome cost, not raw token totals.
  • This gives a short decision report, not a vague observability setup.
  • This works from redacted evidence before any integration work.
  • If the data does not show a leak, the report says that directly.
